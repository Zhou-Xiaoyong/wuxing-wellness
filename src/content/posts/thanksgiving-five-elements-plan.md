Twenty days out, the cart is already half decided. A twenty-pound bird goes in because it is priced by the pound and that is the number people read first. A bag of marshmallows goes in because the sweet potato casserole has always had them. Nobody in the group text is bringing a vegetable that isn't sweet.

Thanksgiving is worth reading through the five phases before the shopping is finished rather than after. The menu is the most concentrated Earth event on the American calendar: sweet, rich, soft, and served across six hours. The furnace comes on in the same week, which is Metal's half of the problem. Two systems, seven days.

What follows is a traditional framework for planning that week, with numbers where the tradition has none. It is not medical advice. Heartburn that now wakes you up, swelling in the legs, or blood sugar you manage with medication belong to a clinician, not a holiday plate.

## One table, four flavors, and the one that is missing

Run a finger down a traditional menu and count the flavors.

Sweet is everywhere: candied yams, marshmallow, brown sugar in the squash, cranberry sauce cooked to jam, three kinds of pie. Salty is the gravy and the butter under the skin. Pungent is the sage, the onion, the pepper. Sour appears once, in the cranberries, and only if nobody has sweetened them into dessert.

Bitter is absent. That is not an accident of American home cooking, but it is the gap that matters, because of one line in the chapter on the qi of the organs and the seasons (《素问·脏气法时论》): the spleen wants to be slackened, sweet slackens it, and bitter drains it (脾欲缓，急食甘以缓之，用苦泻之，甘补之).

Read that as an instruction. Sweet is the spleen's tonic, which is why a soft, warm, sweet meal feels comforting when you are depleted. It is also why a table of nothing but sweet leaves the organ without a counterweight. Bitter is the drain, and nobody puts it on a Thanksgiving table.

The fix is one dish, and it takes ten minutes. Radicchio, endive, or arugula, dressed sharp, placed between the casserole and the pie. Nobody will call it the best thing they ate. Some will eat it, and the plate stops being one flavor deep.

Cranberries are the other lever, and they are already on the list. The back of a standard bag calls for a cup of sugar to twelve ounces of berries. Two-thirds of a cup, the zest of an orange, and a pinch of salt keeps the sauce sour enough to cut the gravy, which is the only job it has.

## How much, and what goes in the fridge on Sunday

The USDA's working rule is a pound of turkey per person once the bird is over twelve pounds, so ten people want a twelve- to fourteen-pound turkey with leftovers. Two extra pounds is a week of sandwiches. Eight extra pounds is a week of guilt.

Buy the bird now, then move it to the fridge on the Sunday before, not Tuesday. Thawing runs twenty-four hours for every four to five pounds, so a bird that size needs three full days, and a Tuesday start leaves it icy in the middle on Thanksgiving morning.

Roasting times on the label are roughly right: 325°F, three to three and three-quarter hours for a bird that size, unstuffed, until the thigh reads 165°F. Stuffing baked in a dish rather than the cavity is the single change that does most for safety and timing, because a stuffed bird has to overcook its outside to cook its middle.

Two things to buy early, one late. Dried shiitake keep for a year and put a savory floor under bread stuffing; a handful, soaked and chopped, is the difference between stuffing and wet bread. A humidifier is worth buying before Black Friday, when every model is gone or marked up. The bitter greens are the late purchase; they will not survive Wednesday.

## The house is the other half of the week

This is the part nobody plans. A heated house in a cold climate commonly sits between 20 and 35 percent relative humidity once the windows close, and the EPA's recommended range is 30 to 50. A house at 78°F and 22 percent is hot and dry at once, the least comfortable combination available, and the one most American Thanksgivings are held in.

The tradition has a distinction that fits November. Through September the dryness is warm (温燥), and cooling pears and chrysanthemum are the right answer. After the cold-dew solar term in early October it turns to cool dryness (凉燥), and the answer becomes warmth plus moisture. That is why the pear soup that fixed your throat in September does less for it in late November.

So: a humidifier in the bedroom, not the living room, because you spend eight hours there with your mouth open. The thermostat down two degrees. A pot of something on the stove. And keep the Wednesday before light, since [Autumn Pear Soup](/recipes/autumn-lung-pear-soup/) beats a second casserole the night before a four-hour roast.

## Four timings that matter more than the menu

**Breakfast, even though the bird is the point.** The organ clock puts the stomach at 7 to 9 a.m. and the spleen an hour behind it, when this framework expects the day's real digestive work to begin. A plate arriving at four meets a system idle since last night. Eggs and toast at nine will not spoil anything.

**Fifteen minutes of walking after the meal, before the pie.** The spleen is assigned the limbs in this framework (脾主四肢), and movement is part of how food moves along. A loop around the block is enough. The point is not exercise.

**Warm water before dessert.** Not a fourth glass of wine, which is warming on the way in and drying by midnight.

**The 2 a.m. wake-up already has a name.** A chapter of the Inner Canon puts it in six characters: when the stomach is not settled, sleep is not settled (胃不和则卧不安，《素问·逆调论》). If you wake at two with a full belly and a dry mouth, the reading is not a sleep problem. It is an amount problem, and the answer is earlier and smaller, not a pill.

## What to skip this year

**The marshmallow top.** A sweet potato is already sweet. Salt, brown butter, and a hot oven give you more flavor than sugar does, and the casserole stops being a second dessert.

**The Friday cleanse.** There is no traditional version of it. The tradition's answer to a heavy meal is a lighter one, not a punitive one, and plain warm food on Friday does the same work without the theater.

**Grazing from two o'clock.** The meal is not the problem. Six hours of cheese, crackers, and dip ahead of it means the stomach never finishes one round before the next arrives.

Start with the bitter dish and the Sunday thaw. Both cost almost nothing this week and change the day more than any menu upgrade. If the heartburn is new, if swelling does not go down overnight, or if the tiredness after eating has been there since summer, that belongs in an exam room rather than a kitchen.

## Quick self-check

| Statement | Feels like me |
| --- | --- |
| I feel heavy and slow for a day or two after a big holiday meal | ☐ |
| My Thanksgiving plate has no bitter or vinegary element on it | ☐ |
| I skip breakfast that day and then eat from mid-afternoon | ☐ |
| I wake around 2 a.m. with a full stomach after a large dinner | ☐ |
| My skin, throat, or eyes are drier indoors once the heat is on | ☐ |
| I buy the turkey that week and thaw it in a hurry | ☐ |

Four or more and both halves of the week are yours to plan. Two or fewer, and the table is probably fine, though the room may still be worth a humidifier.
