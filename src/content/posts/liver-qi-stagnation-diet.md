Ask what to do about a stressed, tight, irritable system and you will rarely hear the word "detox" from a practitioner of Chinese medicine. You will hear something that translates better than it usually gets credit for: soothe the liver (疏肝, shū gān). The image is physical. In this tradition the liver is the organ that keeps the body's energy moving in smooth, even strokes. When life blocks that movement — deadlines, swallowed words, a commute that is really a slow argument — the energy does not leave. It pools. The pooled version is called liver qi stagnation (肝气郁结, gān qì yù jié), and the food approach is simply to give that pooled energy somewhere to go.

This is a traditional self-care framework, not medical advice, and not a substitute for a clinician — especially not for a diagnosed liver condition or persistent pain. Read everything below as cultural practice with a surprisingly modern feel: a ritual, not a treatment.

## What stagnation means at the table

Wood (木, mù) is the phase of spring, upward growth, green, and the flavor Sour (酸, suān). Its natural state is movement. The dietary logic follows directly: you eat in a way that supports flow and you ease off the things that, in this model, clog or inflame it. That is almost the whole philosophy — the food is a channel, not a prescription.

The pattern the diet is aimed at is recognizable to a lot of desk-bound, deadline-driven people: a band of tightness under the ribs, sighing you do not notice, a short fuse, and digestion that worsens on high-friction days. Food will not fix the deadline. But it can stop adding to the problem, and it can gently nudge Wood back toward motion.

## The foods that keep Wood moving

Two flavors do most of the work, and they are the ones Wood naturally likes.

**Sour (酸) gathers and unbinds.** The classics describe sour as the flavor that both collects and releases — exactly the action you want for energy that has balled up. Practical sources: lemon, a little rice vinegar or apple-cider vinegar, pickled greens, plums, and sour cherries. A few sips of warm lemon water in the morning is the flavor-level version of the whole idea.

**Green is Wood's color.** Leafy greens, sprouts, celery, and the bitter spring greens are the visual stand-ins for the phase itself. The tradition reads green foods as gently moving and clearing — exactly the direction a stuck liver wants to go.

A handful of specific traditional allies worth knowing:

- **Rose buds (玫瑰花, méi guī huā).** The classic flower for gently moving stuck liver energy. Our [Rose & Citrus Tea](/recipes/rose-tea-liver/) is built around it.
- **Chrysanthemum flowers (菊花, jú huā).** Used for the heat that builds behind stagnation — the flushed, irritable, wired end of the pattern.
- **Goji berries (枸杞, gǒu qǐ).** A mild, nourishing addition that supports the liver and eyes without adding heat.
- **Mung beans (绿豆, lǜ dòu).** Traditionally used in spring to clear mild internal heat and keep things light.
- **Leafy bitters** — dandelion greens, endive, radicchio — which drain heat downward rather than feeding it.

## What to ease off

Just as important as what to add is what to stop adding, because several common foods are read in this system as either heating the already-hot Wood or clogging the channel it needs.

- **Alcohol.** Classified as heating and as something that knots the liver's smooth flow — the textbook aggravator of the exact pattern described here.
- **Heavily spiced or fried food.** Adds internal heat that, in the model, makes Wood flare rather than flow.
- **Late, heavy meals.** A rushed or greasy dinner is the classic Wood-overacting-on-Earth move — stress derailing digestion right when the liver wants to do its nighttime housekeeping.
- **Too much coffee late in the day.** A little is fine; a lot feeds the Fire that drives Wood into excess, and it steals the sleep Wood needs to reset.

None of these is forbidden forever. The tradition is about proportion and timing — a glass of wine with dinner is not the same event as three drinks at midnight.

## A simple daily plate

If the list above feels like a lot, here is the compressed version the tradition would actually endorse — a sour-and-green day:

- **Morning:** warm water with lemon; a handful of greens at breakfast.
- **Midday:** a vinegar-dressed salad (leafy greens, a little citrus) alongside a normal lunch eaten slowly.
- **Afternoon:** a sour fruit — plum or sour cherry — instead of a third coffee.
- **Evening:** a floral tea (rose or chrysanthemum), screens down, lights low.

That is the whole program. The point is repetition, not intensity. Qi moves because you keep giving it channels, not because you ate one heroic salad.

## The sour-and-green principle in practice

The reason sour plus green works together is that they act on the same stuck energy from two sides: sour unbinds what has balled up, green moves what has pooled. You do not need a complicated recipe. A bowl of steamed greens with a sharp vinaigrette is, in this framework, a liver-soothing meal. A rosebud tea in the evening is the same idea in a cup. The [Chrysanthemum & Goji Tea](/recipes/chrysanthemum-tea/) is another easy entry point.

## Where food stops

Food is one channel, not the only one. The same stagnation that a sour-and-green plate helps also answers to movement (a daily walk), expression (the sentence you keep rescheduling), and sleep (the liver's 1–3 a.m. housekeeping shift). The diet makes the other two easier; it does not replace them. Used together, they are the traditional answer to a pattern most modern life quietly manufactures.

## Quick self-check

| Statement | Feels like me |
| --- | --- |
| I feel a band of tightness under my ribs or along my sides | ☐ |
| My digestion worsens on high-stress or high-friction days | ☐ |
| I sigh audibly without noticing I'm doing it | ☐ |
| Greasy, fried, or late heavy meals reliably make me feel worse | ☐ |
| A little sour or green food noticeably lifts my mood | ☐ |
| Alcohol or too much coffee leaves me tight and irritable the next day | ☐ |

Two or more checks means the sour-and-green approach is worth a month of your time. Zero checks means your Wood is already flowing — guard your sleep anyway.
