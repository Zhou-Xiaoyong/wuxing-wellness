You clicked on this because some part of you already suspects the answer. Maybe a friend called you "intense." Maybe you noticed you can't sit through a slow meeting without your jaw tightening. Or maybe you took a five-element quiz, got Wood, and thought — *okay, but what does that actually mean, and is it really me?*

In Chinese medicine, Wood (木, mù) is the element of spring: the green shoot that cracks pavement to reach the sun. It's the energy of growth, planning, and forward motion, and the Yellow Emperor's Inner Canon (《黄帝内经》) ties it to the **liver** (肝, gān) and **gallbladder** (胆, dǎn) — the organ systems said to keep energy flowing and decisions firm.

One thing before we start: this is a framework for self-reflection, drawn from a tradition more than two thousand years old. It's not a diagnosis, and nothing here is medical advice. Think of it as a mirror, not a chart at the doctor's office.

## The unmistakable Wood tells

Every element leaves fingerprints. Wood's are hard to miss once you know where to look:

- **You're happiest with a plan.** Not necessarily a rigid one — but a direction. Aimlessness feels physically uncomfortable to you.
- **Obstacles make you angry before they make you sad.** Where a Water type withdraws and an Earth type worries, a Wood type heats up. The Inner Canon assigns Wood the emotion of anger (怒, nù) for a reason.
- **You're decisive — sometimes too fast.** The gallbladder, in the traditional view, governs decision-making. Strong Wood types rarely agonize; they choose and go.
- **Injustice is your trigger.** Nothing spikes your pulse like an unfair rule, a broken promise, or someone cutting the line.
- **You'd rather lead than wait.** Even in small things — you're the one who picks the restaurant when the group stalls.

If you nodded through most of those, you're already deep in Wood territory.

## What your body says when Wood runs hot

Temperament is only half the picture. Chinese medicine reads the element in the body too, and Wood has a distinctive physical signature — especially when its energy gets stuck, a pattern called **liver qi stagnation** (肝气郁结, gān qì yù jié):

- Tight shoulders and neck, or a band of pressure along the ribs
- Tension headaches, often behind the eyes or at the temples
- Frequent sighing — your body's own pressure valve
- Waking between 1 and 3 a.m., the liver's hours on the traditional body clock
- A short fuse on days when plans keep slipping

None of this means anything is broken. In the traditional reading, it means your Wood energy — which *wants* to move — has hit a wall. And Wood hates walls more than anything.

## Wood in relationships and at work

Wood types are the engines of any group. You start projects, set the pace, and pull people toward a vision they didn't know they had. The shadow side: patience is not your natural gift. Slow colleagues can read as obstacles; cautious partners can read as brakes.

The traditional advice isn't to blunt the drive — it's to give it channels. A Wood type with a hard workout, a garden to wrangle, or a project with a real deadline is a joy to be around. A Wood type with nowhere to push becomes the person who reorganizes the fridge at midnight out of sheer frustration.

If you recognize yourself here, the kindest thing you can do isn't "calm down." It's **schedule movement the way other people schedule rest**. A brisk walk when a plan stalls does more for a Wood system than an hour of forced stillness.

## Small rituals that suit a Wood constitution

Chinese tradition supports each element with flavor, color, and rhythm rather than prescriptions. For Wood:

- **A touch of sour** (酸, suān) — the Wood flavor. Lemon in water, a vinegar-dressed salad, pickled vegetables. Tradition says Sour gathers scattered energy back in.
- **Green food, daily.** Leafy greens are Wood's color and, in the traditional framing, help "cool" a system that runs hot with drive.
- **Something floral in the evening.** Rose buds (玫瑰花, méi guī huā) are the classic pick for moving stuck liver qi — our [Rose & Citrus Tea for Wood](/recipes/rose-tea-liver) is built around exactly this idea. Call it a ritual, not a treatment.
- **Stretch the sides.** The liver channel runs along the ribs; side-body stretches are the traditional release for Wood tension.
