At 9:40 on the Wednesday before Thanksgiving there are three bowls in the sink, a fork standing upright in the stuffing pan, and a note taped to the fridge door that says DO NOT OPEN. The bird is in a cooler on the porch with a bag of ice on top. Nobody in the house has eaten dinner. Nobody has noticed.

Thursday's menu was settled nine days ago. Wednesday's dinner was never on it. It gets assembled at the counter out of whatever did not fit: a corner of sweet potato, the end of a roll, two split deviled eggs. Then the kitchen gets wiped down, the alarm gets set for six, and the holiday starts with someone who has been awake four hours before the coffee finishes.

This is the last part of the week with room to change. The tradition reads it as one question: how you handle the last meal before a very large one.

None of this is medical advice. If you take a sleep medication, treat reflux, or snore hard enough that someone has mentioned it, the night before a holiday is not the moment to experiment.

## Nineteen hours, and no protein in the middle

Run the arithmetic on the usual version. Picked-at counter food at nine, then a plate at 3:30 the next afternoon. That is eighteen and a half hours with roughly three hundred calories of fat and salt in the middle of it and almost no protein anywhere.

Two things fail in that stretch, and neither is the one people worry about.

The first is the night. A kitchen that runs until eleven means bed at 11:40 with a 5:30 alarm in front of it, which is under six hours. The second is the morning, which holds Thursday's real work: bird dried and salted, oven at 325°F by eight, stuffing in before nine if it is going to be safe. Those jobs go badly on six hours.

One more cost shows up late. A cook who tastes from noon on Wednesday and stands for eleven hours on Thursday has eaten small rich things for two days with no interval in it. Friday is when that arrives.

## An unsettled stomach is a bad sleeping pill

Ask a classical text about insomnia and the answer is not about the mind. The Inner Canon (《黄帝内经》) files it under digestion in the chapter on disorders of regulation, in six characters: when the stomach is not settled, sleep is not settled (胃不和则卧不安).

The reasoning is not mystical. Lying down within two hours of a large rich meal puts a full stomach on a horizontal plane, and the valve at the top of it is not built for that. Most people who wake at 1:30 after a late dinner are not anxious. They are being refluxed at a level they never register.

The seasonal chapter gives the other half of the instruction for late autumn: early to bed and early to rise, in step with the rooster (早卧早起，与鸡俱兴). It is the least popular sentence in Chinese medicine, because it asks for an hour at the front of the night rather than the back.

The workable version for a Wednesday in November is three hours between the last real food and lights out. If bed is at 10:30, the bowl belongs at 7:30. Eight o'clock is the compromise most people can hold, and it still leaves two and a half hours.

## What to eat at eight, and what not to drink at nine

The meal is not a project. Four hundred calories, warm, soft enough to eat without thinking, one bowl, sitting down.

Half a cup of dry millet cooked with five cups of water and three slices of fresh ginger (生姜, shēng jiāng) is about 300 calories and mostly water by weight. It takes thirty-five to forty-five minutes, roughly one oven cycle, so it cooks while the bird comes up to room temperature. Add a boiled egg for twelve grams of protein. The [ginger and millet congee](/recipes/congee-settled-stomach/) is the same bowl Thursday morning wants.

If the stove is a wall of foil by eight, broth with toast and two eggs does the same work, or the [autumn pear soup](/recipes/autumn-lung-pear-soup/) if a batch is already cold. What matters is the shape: warm, soft, protein present, eaten off a plate.

The drink carries more cost than people price in. A first glass at six is not the problem. The second at nine is, because alcohol shortens the time it takes to fall asleep and then takes it back after midnight, when REM rebounds and the last three hours come apart. Two five-ounce glasses is enough. The 11 p.m. glass finished at the sink turns a seven-hour night into a five-hour one.

## Ten o'clock is about the house, not the kitchen

By ten the cooking is done or it is not going to be, and two things are left.

Drop the thermostat three degrees, to 68°F. The oven has been on since five and the kitchen will sit near 78 for two more hours, and that heat goes upstairs. Sleep onset depends on core temperature falling. Crack one window an inch. Heated indoor air in late November routinely sits under 30 percent relative humidity, dry enough to leave a mouth breather with a raw throat by Friday, which is the pattern the tradition calls autumn dryness (秋燥). A pot of water on the back burner for twenty minutes beats a humidifier bought in a panic on Wednesday.

Then the paper. Serving dishes out, empty. Thursday's oven times on an index card stuck to the hood: bird in, bird out, rest, stuffing, pie. Four minutes, and it removes the 6 a.m. conversation where two people rebuild a timeline while holding a raw turkey.

That is Metal's half of the holiday, and the eve is where it belongs: closing, putting away, deciding what is not happening. The table on Thursday is Earth. Wednesday night is where you stop adding to it.

## If it is already midnight

Most people will lose Wednesday anyway, so here is the version for 12:20 a.m. with the bird still in the sink.

Cut the menu to four things: the bird, one starch, one sour or bitter vegetable, the pie. One of the rest is not getting made, so choose it now instead of at eleven on Thursday. Buy the rolls. Buy the pie if the pie is what stands between you and six hours.

Then sleep six hours rather than four and a half. Forty minutes of getting ahead at midnight comes out of the only part of the day that cannot be recovered. If you have already eaten standing up and slept five, do not skip breakfast to compensate. That move is covered in [whether to eat breakfast on Thanksgiving](/blog/should-you-eat-breakfast-thanksgiving/), and it makes the plate land worse.

One bad night is survivable. Three across a holiday week ends with a sore throat on Monday and a story about how the holidays always make you sick.

Reflux that wakes you repeatedly, snoring with pauses in it, or a night broken for months is not a scheduling problem. Those belong to a clinician, and the holiday is a fine time to mention them.

Set the bowl and the bedtime tonight, while the kitchen is calm enough to hear yourself think. If Wednesday runs past eleven anyway, eat standing, go to bed, and protect Thursday's first hour instead of its last.

## Quick self-check

| Statement | Feels like me |
| --- | --- |
| I have not decided what I'm eating on Wednesday night | ☐ |
| My last food before Thanksgiving comes off a sheet pan, standing up | ☐ |
| I will probably still be in the kitchen at eleven on Wednesday | ☐ |
| My bedroom will be over 72°F with the window shut | ☐ |
| There is a second glass of wine in the plan, and it lands after nine | ☐ |
| Nobody in the house has written down Thursday's oven times | ☐ |

Four or more and Wednesday night is worth ten minutes of planning, which is more than it has ever been given. Two or fewer, and the only thing left is to tell whoever is brining the bird that the cooler goes on the porch, not in the guest room.
