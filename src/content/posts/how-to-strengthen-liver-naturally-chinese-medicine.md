Your eyes start watering on the short walk to the car, and by four in the afternoon the print in front of you has gone soft at the edges. You slept seven hours. Nothing hurts. Last spring's bloodwork came back normal, and the iron pills from March are still in the cupboard because the second one made you sick.

Ask what is wrong with your liver and the internet hands back a familiar list: too much fat, too much wine, too much anger swallowed. Clean it out. Add lemon. None of that is wrong, exactly. It is aimed at one of the two ways Wood fails, and if you are in the other one, it makes the week worse.

In the five-element system the liver (肝, gān) carries two jobs that pull in opposite directions. Most English-language writing has dropped one of them. What follows is the dropped half, plus a way to tell whether it is yours. It is not medical advice. Anemia, iron overload and liver disease are diagnosed by blood tests and imaging, not by a page like this one.

## The liver is where blood goes when you lie down

The line sits in the chapter of the Inner Canon (《黄帝内经》) that traces each organ to what it produces, and it is one of the most physical sentences in the book: when a person lies down, blood returns to the liver (人卧血归于肝).

It does not stop there. Four verbs follow. The liver receiving blood, the eyes can see. The feet receiving it, you can walk. The palm, you can grip. The fingers, you can pinch (《素问·五脏生成篇》).

Read that as a supply chain, not as poetry. Four ordinary abilities downstream of one delivery, and not one word about clearing anything out.

Which is why the tradition's word for a weak liver is not sluggish. It is empty (肝血虚, gān xuè xū, liver blood deficiency), and the signs gather where the passage points: the eyes, the nails (其华在爪, its flower is in the nails), the sinews (肝主筋, the liver governs the sinews, 《素问·宣明五气篇》). A nail that splits down the middle is the far end of a long pipe.

## Two failures, and only one of them is stuck

Here is the split that matters. An 18th-century clinical manual, Ye Tianshi's case records (《临证指南医案》), gives the liver a four-character summary later practitioners treat as the key to the whole organ: yin in substance, yang in function (体阴而用阳, tǐ yīn ér yòng yáng).

What the liver is made of is blood, heavy and still. What it does is move, quick and light. Two materials, two ways to fail.

A stuck liver is a movement problem. Tight shoulders, sighing, a short fuse, a band of pressure under the ribs. The other Wood pages here live there, and the routine is sound: walk, stretch, a little sour.

An empty liver is a supply problem, and more movement drains it. Whoever takes up the brisk morning walk and the lemon water and ends the week flatter than she started has been handed the wrong half.

## The 1 a.m. wake-up is real. The detox story is not.

Wood's window on the organ clock is 1 to 3 a.m., and nearly every page that mentions it says the liver is filtering then, so waking means it is struggling. That is a modern addition. The clock itself (子午流注, zǐ wǔ liú zhù) comes from a 12th-century needling manual, the Ziwu Liuzhu Zhenjing (《子午流注针经》), printed in 1153. It scheduled needles, not organ function.

What the classical line does support is quieter. Blood returns to the liver during rest, and specifically during lying down. That makes the first ninety minutes after you get horizontal the part that counts, and turns a 1 a.m. wake-up into a statement about the depth of the night rather than the state of the organ.

The lever is the hour you go to bed, not the hour you wake. Being upright and lit at 11:40 p.m. is not neutral here.

## At the table, the arithmetic beats the food list

Food builds the store, and the useful part is not another list of iron-rich ingredients. It is absorption: plenty of people eat enough iron and take in very little.

Two forms behave differently. Heme iron, from meat and fish, arrives in a package the gut absorbs efficiently. Non-heme iron, from beans, greens and dried fruit, has to be converted first and competes with everything else on the plate. Three things decide the outcome.

**Vitamin C at the same meal.** It can double or triple non-heme absorption, and 100 mg is about half a bell pepper or one small orange.

**Tannins in tea and coffee.** A strong cup alongside a meal can cut non-heme absorption by roughly half. The old habit of tea between meals rather than with them has a mechanism under it. Give it an hour.

**Calcium.** Milk or cheese at the same sitting competes for the same doorway, which is a reason to move it to another meal rather than to skip it.

One convergence, without overclaiming: the human liver holds roughly ninety percent of the body's vitamin A, and the organ the tradition ties to the eyes is the same one. Anatomy happening to agree, not proof of a doctrine.

The canon adds a flavor instruction: the liver suffers from urgency, and sweet slackens it (肝苦急，急食甘以缓之, 《素问·脏气法时论》). Sweet as food, not candy. A roasted sweet potato, a spoon of honey, rice with dinner.

## The number to count is your comeback time

One chapter gives the liver a title about strength rather than detox: the root of the extreme of fatigue (肝者，罢极之本, 《素问·六节藏象论》). The pair of characters (罢极, pí jí) names the point at which exertion runs out. The liver is not the muscle. It sets how much load you carry before the tank reads empty.

That gives you a measurement, cheap and specific. Climb the same flight at the same pace, then time how long until your breathing and your legs are back to normal. Not how hard the climb was. How long the return took. Once a week, same stairs, same hour, written down.

Two levers, cheapest first.

**Be horizontal before eleven, five nights out of seven.** Earlier than feels necessary on the first night, because the first night is not the one being tested.

**Move the last cup of tea an hour off the iron meal.** Same cup, different slot, no discipline required.

Then count the comeback. One line in a notebook. A change of thirty seconds is worth reading; a single bad week is the week.

Start with the comeback number. It produces a figure and asks nothing of your willpower. If it has not moved in three weeks, or if the tiredness arrives with heavy periods, black stools, breathlessness on flat ground, or a sudden urge to chew ice, put the notebook down and ask for a ferritin test and a complete blood count. Do not start iron on the strength of a blog post. Too much iron is its own disease, and roughly one in two hundred people of northern European descent carries two copies of the gene that causes it.

## Quick self-check

| Statement | Feels like me |
| --- | --- |
| My nails split or peel, and they used to be smooth | ☐ |
| My eyes go dry and gritty by late afternoon | ☐ |
| I wake between 1 and 3 a.m. and stay awake a while | ☐ |
| My calves cramp at night, or my hamstrings never quite loosen | ☐ |
| I see spots or grey out when I stand up quickly | ☐ |
| Ordinary effort takes longer to recover from than it did last year | ☐ |

Four or more, and the empty half is worth working on before the moving half. Two or fewer, and the surplus routine in the links below is the better fit. Rows two and six are the pair to watch: both answer to sleep before they answer to anything you can buy.
