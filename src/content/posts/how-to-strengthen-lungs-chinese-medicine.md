The call is going fine until the last four words of your sentence, which arrive with no air behind them. You finish at half volume and hope it passed for emphasis.

You are not winded the way a hill winds you. You are short of the thing that pushes the sentence.

The tradition has a name for that thing, and it is not lung capacity. It is a qi no single organ can produce, which makes "strengthen your lungs" half an instruction, and the missing half is usually the one that needs the work.

What follows is a traditional reading of that mechanism and two numbers you can take with a phone timer. This is not medical advice. Breathlessness has a long list of causes, and a few are cardiac and belong to a doctor rather than a teapot.

## The one qi that takes two organs to make

One passage of the Inner Canon (《黄帝内经》) describes an assembly line in about thirty characters. Five grains go into the stomach, and what comes out is sorted into three channels: waste, fluids, and gathering qi (宗气, zōng qì). That qi collects in the chest, leaves by the throat, threads the heart's vessels, and drives the breath (《灵枢·邪客》).

Read the order of operations. Grain first. Air is not what that sentence begins with.

The pool it collects in has an address, the middle of the chest (膻中, dàn zhōng), which the same canon calls the sea of qi (《灵枢·海论》). A sea, not a tank.

So a lung-only approach to a thin voice and a short breath works one of two supply lines. Classical practice named the repair: bank the earth to generate metal (培土生金, péi tǔ shēng jīn). Earth is the spleen, metal the lung, and the phrase describes a handoff, since the digestive center refines food into the qi the lung then combines with air. Cut either supply and the chest has less to work with, no matter how deeply you breathe.

## It leaves the body in two places, so take two numbers

That same passage gives gathering qi two exits: the throat and the heart's vessels. Most classical concepts have to be inferred. This one can be tested in two places at once, and the readings can disagree, which is the useful part.

**The throat.** Sustaining a steady vowel is a load test of the exhale. Take a comfortable breath, say "ahh" at ordinary pitch and volume, and time it. Healthy adults hold 15 to 25 seconds; speech-language pathologists start looking below 10. The mechanism is subglottal pressure, the air under closed vocal folds. It is also why the end of a long sentence fades.

**The vessels.** The lung receives the whole output of the right ventricle, about 5 liters a minute at rest, before that blood goes anywhere else. The canon packed it into four characters, the lung faces the hundred vessels (肺朝百脉, fèi cháo bǎi mài), in the Plain Questions (《素问·经脉别论》). Which makes the pulse response to a fixed climb a second reading of the same assembly.

Climb one flight at ordinary speed. Stop. Time how long until your breathing is unremarkable, then take your pulse again a minute later. In a 1999 study of 2,428 adults, a heart rate that fell 12 beats or fewer in that first minute tracked with higher mortality over the years that followed. Not a diagnosis. A direction of travel.

## The place the canon says you can see it

Nearly everything in this system is inferred from the outside. Gathering qi has one exception, and the passage is oddly specific about the address: the great collateral of the stomach, called the empty mile (虚里, xū lǐ), emerges below the left breast, where its movement stirs the clothing, and gathering qi is leaking (《素问·平人气象论》).

Anatomy calls that spot the apex beat. A visible impulse there is common in thin people with normal hearts, and the canon treats the *visible* version as the leak rather than the palpable one. A sustained heave that lifts the rib cage is a different finding, and it belongs to a cardiologist.

## The half that goes missing first is not the air

Here is the correction worth the read. You cannot train your lungs bigger. Total lung capacity is set mostly by height, age and sex, and in healthy non-smokers the volume you can force out in one second falls about 30 milliliters a year after thirty. Swimmers move the edges of that, not much, and not through breathing exercises.

So the tank is fixed and the exits are not. What responds is the machinery: the diaphragm and the muscles between the ribs, skeletal muscle that loses mass on the same schedule as a thigh, plus the circulation carrying what the lung loaded.

That is where the grain half reaches a modern kitchen. Protein needs rise with age rather than fall, and 1.0 to 1.2 grams per kilogram is where geriatric nutrition groups land past 65. For a 160-pound person that is 73 to 87 grams a day, and breakfast is where it collapses: coffee and something from a bag supplies about 6.

The tradition's formula for this exact picture, breath short, voice thin, limbs heavy, is Return to the Middle to Support Qi (补中益气汤, bǔ zhōng yì qì tāng), recorded by Li Dongyuan in 1249 (《脾胃论》). Its chief herb is astragalus (黄芪, huáng qí), and its logic is the phrase from earlier: work the earth to lift the metal. It is a prescription with a dose, not a grocery decision, so it comes from a practitioner.

One honest note. Breathlessness on stairs in an otherwise well adult is usually deconditioning, not lung disease, and the fix is the same flight of stairs, repeated.

## Eight weeks, two numbers

Choose one flight you climb every week and always at the same pace. Time the recovery, the second your breathing stops being audible. Then do the vowel, three tries, best one counts. Write both numbers down with the date.

Repeat at eight weeks. If the vowel time climbs and the stair recovery does not, the throat end is answering while the circulation lags. If recovery improves and the vowel does not, you gained fitness and left the grain half alone. Two numbers that disagree say more than one that moves.

Feel is unreliable across seasons, and eight weeks is long enough for a real change and short enough to still be about the thing you changed. A bowl of [pear soup](/recipes/autumn-lung-pear-soup/) on a dry evening makes a reasonable companion to the routine.

Caveats, and they are not decorative. A vowel time under 10 seconds with any change in voice quality is a question for an ENT. Breathlessness at rest, breathlessness lying flat, or breathlessness that arrived over weeks rather than years is a doctor this week. And if you smoke, none of the above outranks stopping.

## Quick self-check

| Statement | Feels like me |
| --- | --- |
| The last word of a sentence drops off before I mean it to | ☐ |
| I can hold a steady "ahh" for less than 10 seconds | ☐ |
| My legs outlast my breathing on a flight of stairs | ☐ |
| My heart is still going a full minute after I stop climbing | ☐ |
| Breakfast is coffee and something that came from a bag | ☐ |
| Talking all day costs me my voice before it costs me energy | ☐ |

Four or more, and take the two numbers before you take any supplement. Two or fewer, and the fading at the end of your sentences is probably the phone line.
