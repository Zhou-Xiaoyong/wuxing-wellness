It is 2:40 in the morning and you can feel the beat in the pillow before you can feel anything else. Or it is 7:10 and the watch says 64, seven beats above what it said in June. Neither one hurts. Both are the same signal.

Fire, in this framework, is not a temperature. It is circulation and contact: the phase the tradition hands to the heart and to the vessels that carry its work outward (心主血脉, xīn zhǔ xuè mài). Warm palms, color in the face, an easy laugh, sleep that starts on time. Everything else about Fire follows from that.

Which gives Fire what the other four do not have: a gauge you already own.

What follows is a traditional reading of that gauge and the things that move it. It is not medical advice and it diagnoses nothing. A resting rate that stays above 100, a beat that skips, chest pain, breathlessness on the stairs, or fainting are questions for a clinician, not for this page.

## The one phase with an instrument built in

The chapter on the subtle points of the pulse (《素问·脉要精微论》) opens with a line that reads like a specification sheet: the vessels are the house of blood, a long pulse means the qi is orderly, a short one means it is disordered, and a rapid one means the heart is agitated (脉者，血之府也。长则气治，短则气病，数则烦心).

That last clause is the useful one. It is not saying a fast pulse is a disease. It is saying the rate carries a reading of the heart's state the way a tachometer carries a reading of an engine. Two fingers inside the wrist, sixty seconds, and you have a number that answers to last night by this morning. Almost nothing else you can measure at home responds that fast.

## The classical normal is a ceiling, not a goal

The same book defines a healthy person by arithmetic. In the chapter on the pulse of the ordinary man (《素问·平人气象论》), two beats come on the out-breath, two on the in-breath, and a fifth in the pause between them, five across a full breath, and that is the ordinary person (人一呼脉再动，一吸脉再动，呼吸定息脉五动，闰以太息，命曰平人).

Run the numbers on a normal breathing rate and the classical gate sits near 80. Modern guidance is wider: the American Heart Association calls 60 to 100 a normal resting rate.

Both figures describe the range that keeps you out of a clinic, not a place to aim for. Treating 80 as fine is how a person spends three years at the top of a band that cohort studies associate with worse outcomes at the low end. The number worth tracking is your own, taken the same way on three consecutive days.

## Fast Fire and low Fire do not feel alike

The rate tells you Fire is off. Warmth and sleep tell you which direction.

| | Fire running high | Fire running low |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Resting pulse | high 70s and up, easy to find | high 50s or low 60s, thin under the finger |
| Hands and feet | warm, sometimes hot at night | cold all day, colder by evening |
| Sleep | falls asleep fast, wakes between two and four | lies awake at eleven, unable to start |
| Face | flushes in a warm room or after wine | pale, and you feel the cold indoors |
| What helps | descent, an earlier last meal, a cooler room | warmth, cooked food, rest, morning light |

Read the sleep row twice. Waking at three with a dry mouth is a full tank with nowhere to put the fuel. Lying awake at eleven with cold feet is a system that never lit. They arrive looking like the same complaint, and only one improves when you cut the afternoon coffee.

## What turns the dial up

**The evening drink.** Alcohol lifts sleeping heart rate by several beats a minute. The effect grows with the dose and runs most of the night, so a glass at seven is still in the reading at three.

**A meal after nine.** Digestion has a cost. A large late one keeps the rate up for two or three hours after you lie down, which is the whole window you needed it low.

**A bedroom at 76 degrees.** Sleep onset depends on core temperature falling. Most sleep guidance lands in the mid-60s, and heating the whole house in November fights that directly.

**A screen at eleven.** In a 2015 experiment, adults reading on a tablet for four hours before bed had melatonin arrive about ninety minutes late, at roughly half the usual amount. The light is the problem, not the content.

**A hard session at eight in the evening.** The training is fine. Ending it two hours before bed leaves the rate high exactly when it should be dropping.

## What turns it back down

**Ten minutes outdoors within an hour of waking.** The clock that schedules the nightly drop is set in the morning. Cheapest lever on the page, and the one people skip.

**Fifteen minutes of walking after the last meal.** Not for calories. The point is to finish the day's digestion before the day ends.

**A room two degrees cooler.** Dark, too. The body reads both before it reads the clock.

**A longer out-breath than in-breath.** Exhalation is the only part of the cycle you can slow on purpose, and slowing it pulls the rate down inside a minute or two. No count is required.

## When Fire is low, cooling is the wrong tool

Most Fire writing in English is about heat, and there is a reason: summer is Fire's season, and the tradition says so in one phrase (《素问·六节藏象论》: 通于夏气).

November is not that. No seasonal support to draw on, so whatever Fire you have is reserve. That is why a person who was comfortable all July can feel flat, cold-handed and unmotivated by November, and why the reflex answer of more raw food, iced drinks and a January cleanse moves them further the wrong way.

The low version wants the opposite: cooked food, warmth at the wrists and ankles, an earlier night, and something building rather than something bitter and draining. [Longan and Lily Tea](/recipes/longan-lily-tea/) is the traditional night cup for this pattern, sweet and blood-building rather than cooling.

## Three mornings, one number, one lever

- Count for a full sixty seconds, lying down, before you sit up and before you touch a phone. Three mornings running.
- Write the three numbers down. Their average is your baseline. No single reading is.
- Pick one lever from the two lists above, the largest one you can hold. For most people that is the evening drink or the meal after nine.
- Re-measure after ten days, same way, same hour.

A change of four beats or more is worth reading. Two or three is noise, and chasing noise is how people end up with a drawer of supplements and no information.

Start with the three counts, because they cost five minutes and they sort you into one of the two columns above. If a high number stays high for two weeks with no lever moving it, or if it arrives with a flutter, a skipped beat, or breathlessness you did not have last month, stop reading your own pulse and have someone read it properly.

## Quick self-check

| Statement | Feels like me |
| --- | --- |
| I can feel my pulse in my ears or the pillow when I lie down | ☐ |
| My resting rate sits in the 50s or 60s and my hands are cold all day | ☐ |
| My pulse is quick to find and fast after an ordinary day, not a hard one | ☐ |
| I fall asleep easily and then wake between two and four | ☐ |
| I lie awake at eleven even when I am tired, with cold feet | ☐ |
| I have never counted my resting pulse | ☐ |

Rows one, three and four read as a Fire with too much running and not enough descent. Rows two and five read as the opposite, and cooling will make them worse. Row six is the one to fix first.
