Personality quizzes ask how you act at a party. A constitution quiz asks something quieter and, honestly, more useful: what is your body's *default setting*? Do you run hot or cold? Does stress hit your stomach or your shoulders? Do you wake at 3 a.m. or sleep like a stone and still feel drained?

In traditional Chinese wellness this baseline is called your constitution (体质, tǐ zhì) — the pattern you were more or less born with, shaped slowly by age, season, and habit. The five-element version maps that baseline onto Wood, Fire, Earth, Metal, and Water, the same framework the Yellow Emperor's Inner Canon (《黄帝内经》) used to sort people into elemental types over two thousand years ago.

A quick, important note: a constitution quiz is a reflection tool built on a classical framework, not a medical assessment. Nothing here diagnoses anything or replaces a clinician. Use it the way you'd use a good journal prompt — to notice patterns you've been living with but never named.

## Constitution vs. personality: why the difference matters

Your personality can flex with context — plenty of people are Fire at a dinner party and Water on a Monday morning. Constitution moves much more slowly. It shows up in things you *can't* perform:

- Whether your hands and feet run warm or cold
- How your digestion behaves under stress
- What time of night you wake, if you wake
- Whether your skin trends dry, oily, flushed, or pale
- How long you take to recover after a demanding week

That's why a constitution quiz asks about tendencies over months and years, not feelings this week. It's also why the results tend to be more stable — and more practical, since food and seasonal routines in the Chinese tradition are matched to constitution first.

## The five constitutions at a glance

Here's the shorthand the quiz is scoring you against:

- **Wood constitution** — lean, springy build; tension gathers in shoulders and ribs; irritable under pressure; sleep breaks between 1 and 3 a.m. Runs on drive and dislikes being stalled.
- **Fire constitution** — warm hands, quick flush, bright expressive eyes; energy comes in sparks; sleep is light when the mind is excited. Runs on connection and dislikes boredom.
- **Earth constitution** — sturdy, comfort-loving frame; stress lands in the stomach; energy dips after heavy meals; prone to overthinking (思, sī — the Earth emotion) in quiet hours. Runs on routine and dislikes chaos.
- **Metal constitution** — neat, contained bearing; dry skin and a sensitive nose or throat, especially in autumn dryness (秋燥); grief and letting-go sit close to the surface. Runs on order and dislikes mess.
- **Water constitution** — cool hands and feet, deep reserves, dark under-eye circles when depleted; stamina hides under stillness; winter is the season that tests it. Runs on depth and dislikes being rushed.

Most people are a two-element blend — an Earth base with a Fire streak, a Metal frame with Wood ambition. The quiz's job is to find the *lead* element, because that's the one your daily habits should support first.

## How to score yourself honestly

The classic mistake is answering as the person you'd like to be. Constitution lives in the boring facts, so anchor every answer in evidence:

1. **Use the last twelve months, not the last bad week.** One stressful month can make anyone look like a Wood type on paper.
2. **Ask someone who lives with you.** They know whether you actually run cold or just like sweaters.
3. **Weight the body over the mood.** If your temperament says Fire but your cold feet, deep stamina, and love of solitude say Water, trust the body — it has less imagination.
4. **Expect a runner-up.** The second-highest score usually explains every contradiction in your first.

## What to do with your result

The traditional payoff of knowing your constitution is small, seasonal adjustment — not a life overhaul. A Water constitution guards warmth in winter; an Earth constitution eats regular, warm meals instead of grazing cold ones; a Metal constitution humidifies the autumn air and lets go of one thing on purpose. Tiny moves, matched to your baseline, repeated until they're invisible.

That's also the honest limit of the framework: it's a tradition of pattern-reading and self-care, not a treatment plan. If something in your body genuinely worries you, that's a conversation for a licensed professional, not a quiz.

## Quick self-check

| Statement | Feels like me |
| --- | --- |
| My hands and feet have run cold (or hot) for as long as I remember | ☐ |
| Stress reliably hits the same body part — stomach, shoulders, or chest | ☐ |
| My energy pattern is stable across jobs, cities, and relationships | ☐ |
| People who've known me for years describe my temperament consistently | ☐ |
| My sleep breaks (or doesn't) at roughly the same hour, year-round | ☐ |
| Season changes affect me in the same predictable way every year | ☐ |

The more boxes you tick, the more clearly defined your constitution is — and the more useful your quiz result will be.
