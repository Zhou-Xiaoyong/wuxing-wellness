In March you took the quiz on a phone. The screen said Earth, listed an organ and a season, and you screenshotted it. Ask yourself now which element finished second, and by how many points. Most people can't answer. The result screen never told them, and a screenshot of a single word is not a record of anything.

That gap is what a five element personality ebook usually sells: forty pages of type descriptions and six pages of appendix. The appendix is the part you would actually use, so this page is the appendix, in full, with nothing to download.

What follows is a traditional framework for noticing your own patterns. It is not medical advice, not a psychological assessment, and not a diagnosis of anything.

## The test is twenty statements in fixed blocks

The quiz here asks twenty short statements and rates each one from 1 (not me) to 5 (very me). Four statements belong to each element, and they sit in blocks in a set order: statements 1 through 4 are Wood, 5 through 8 are Fire, 9 through 12 are Earth, 13 through 16 are Metal, 17 through 20 are Water.

That order is the whole trick of scoring by hand. Your Wood score is the sum of your four answers to statements 1 to 4. Nothing is weighted, nothing is reversed, and no question belongs to two elements. Each total runs from 4 to 20.

The result screen shows the winner's name, its organ, its flavor, its color, its season. It does not show the other four numbers. That is not a flaw specific to this quiz. Almost every one-click typing tool returns a label instead of a shape, because a label is shareable and a shape is not.

## The tally sheet

Copy this onto paper. Four lines per element is all the arithmetic involved.

| Element | Statements | Your four ratings | Total |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Wood (木) | 1–4 | ___ + ___ + ___ + ___ | |
| Fire (火) | 5–8 | ___ + ___ + ___ + ___ | |
| Earth (土) | 9–12 | ___ + ___ + ___ + ___ | |
| Metal (金) | 13–16 | ___ + ___ + ___ + ___ | |
| Water (水) | 17–20 | ___ + ___ + ___ + ___ | |

The number worth memorizing is 12. Four answers averaging a 3 come to 12, and 3 is the unlabeled middle of the scale, the "neither" you get when a statement doesn't move you either way.

A 12 is not an absence. It means those four questions didn't engage, which is a different finding from "I have no Water in me" and a more useful one. A 4 means four "not me" answers. A 20 usually describes someone who rates generously across the board, not someone with one enormous element.

The raw total on its own says little: two people can score 17 on Fire with quite different profiles underneath.

## When a gap is a gap

With four statements per element, one notch on one question moves a total by a single point. So a two-point lead can be produced by answering a single statement slightly differently on a different day. It is a mood, not a finding.

A four-point lead needs at least four separate notches to appear. That is a pattern.

| Lead over the runner-up | How to read it |
| --- | --- |
| 0–2 points | A tie. Read both type pages, then decide by which one describes your bad weeks. |
| 3–4 points | Your type, with a real secondary. Read the secondary for how you behave under load. |
| 5 or more | Clear type. Read the secondary anyway, once. It is where you go when the first one runs dry. |

One mechanical detail matters before you trust a close call. Rate every statement a 3 and all five totals land on 12. A tie like that still has to produce one name on screen, so the software resolves it by the order the elements are listed in, Wood first. Nothing about you decided it. Write the numbers down and you keep the tie; skip the sheet and the tie becomes a Wood.

## One statement in each block is a strain item

Each element's four statements are three strengths and one strain. Wood's fourth is impatience when things move too slowly. Fire's third is swinging from joy to anxiety. Earth's third is worry and overthinking. Metal's fourth is how hard it is to let go. Water's fourth is holding back from risk.

Now watch the same total read two ways. Someone lists Wood as 5, 4, 3, 2: decisive, quick, steady under pressure, and unbothered by delay. Someone else lists Wood as 2, 3, 4, 5: not especially ambitious, not especially fast, but irritable every time a plan stalls.

Fourteen points each. Opposite people.

The first has momentum that works. The second has momentum that is blocked, and the tradition would read that as liver qi (肝气) not moving rather than as a personality. If your winning element leans on its strain statement, the honest interpretation is that this element is under pressure right now, not that it defines you. Retest in a season and see whether the shape changes.

## What the number cannot do

The test has no published validation behind it, which matters more than it sounds. In 1949 the psychologist Bertram Forer handed students a personality profile assembled from a newsstand astrology column and asked how well it fit. The average rating was 4.26 out of 5. Any short questionnaire that describes a likable, somewhat contradictory person will feel accurate, which is why "this is so me" is not evidence of much.

The classical system was also coarser than the modern quiz and knew it. The Spiritual Pivot (《灵枢》) devotes a chapter, the twenty-five types (阴阳二十五人), to splitting each element into five subtypes by build, complexion and temperament, which gets you twenty-five profiles out of the same five phases. A five-box result is the shallow end of that system by design, not a simplification somebody invented last year.

And it is not a health screen. Low Water does not mean kidney disease. High Fire does not mean hypertension. Those are organs in a philosophical map, not organs on an ultrasound.

## Four lines, one year

A single result cannot show drift, which is the interesting part. The framework already has a calendar for it: spring leans Wood, summer Fire, late summer Earth, autumn Metal, winter Water. Retest at each turn and you have four data points a year.

Write each line in the same order every time, because a shifting order is how a 17 becomes a remembered 12.

> Oct 30 · 15 / 12 / 17 / 11 / 9 · Earth over Wood

If Earth leads in late October and Fire led in July, the difference is the season talking, not a new personality. If Fire leads in January two years running, that is worth a thought.

## Quick self-check

| Statement | Feels like me |
| --- | --- |
| I remember my quiz result but not the numbers behind it | ☐ |
| I've retaken a typing test and got a different answer | ☐ |
| I'd rather see five scores than one label | ☐ |
| I've read a type description and felt it was written for me | ☐ |
| I'd keep a four-line log if someone handed me the format | ☐ |

Three or more and the sheet earns its ten minutes. If only the fourth one is true, read the paragraph on Forer before anything else on this page, because the feeling of being described is the least reliable evidence you have.

## Fill in the first line tonight

Do it while the statements are fresh, and write the second-place number even if the gap looks obvious. That number is the one you will want in February.

If a five-way tie is what you get, don't read it as a balanced soul. Read it as an invitation to go back to the four statements you skimmed, because a flat profile almost always means a rushed one.

One caveat. If what sent you looking is something that has felt wrong for weeks, a typology is the wrong instrument. The sheet is for noticing a pattern in how you meet the day. It has no opinion about why you can't sleep.
