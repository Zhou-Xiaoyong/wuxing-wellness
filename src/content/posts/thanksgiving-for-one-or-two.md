The smallest whole turkey in the case at nine on Tuesday morning weighs fourteen pounds. Fourteen pounds is eleven servings. If there are two of you at the table on Thursday, that bird is a week of sandwiches and a freezer you did not plan to fill.

So the short answer is a three-pound bone-in turkey breast, salted tonight, roasted at 350°F on Thursday for about an hour and a half, pulled at 165°F in the thickest part. Dinner for two with a little left over, and it fits in a toaster oven.

The long answer is the rest of the menu, because nothing else scales down on its own. A stuffing recipe begins with twelve cups of cubed bread. A bag of cranberries makes eight servings of sauce. Thanksgiving is the one American meal whose recipes were all written for a crowd, and doing it small means rewriting them at the counter on Wednesday.

None of this is medical advice. If you are managing diabetes, kidney disease, or a swallow that has been getting harder, portion changes are a conversation for your clinician rather than a paragraph here.

## Every recipe in your browser feeds twelve

Start with the bird, because the bird decides everything else.

A frozen fourteen-pounder needs about three days in the refrigerator to thaw, at roughly five pounds per day. Bought Tuesday morning, it cannot be ready Thursday afternoon by fridge time. The only route left is a cold-water bath at thirty minutes per pound, which is seven hours of changing the water, and a bird that size will not sit flat in most kitchen sinks. The trap: Thursday's turkey was decided on Monday.

A three-pound breast thaws overnight in the refrigerator. It also salts differently. This roast has far more exposed surface per pound than a big bird, so it takes more seasoning per pound, and it gives up moisture faster at the end.

Halve the rest, with one exception. Stuffing for two is three cups of cubed bread, about four or five slices, dried on a sheet pan while the oven preheats, plus half an onion, one celery stalk, two tablespoons of butter, half a cup of broth, and a teaspoon of dried sage. It fits an eight-by-four loaf pan and bakes thirty minutes at 350°F, covered for the first twenty. Cook it beside the bird, never inside it. A small bird with a cold center of bread is how a two-person Thanksgiving ends at urgent care. The exception to halving is seasoning: salt, pepper, sage, and orange peel stay at two-thirds, or the small version tastes like a large one diluted.

## The season's own word for a table that fits

Late autumn has a name in the Inner Canon's chapter on following the seasons. The three months of autumn are called receiving and leveling (容平, róng píng), the point in the year when things settle to their actual measure. The same passage calls this the way of nourishing gathering-in (养收之道, yǎng shōu zhī dào).

That word works against the American instinct here. Twelve dishes is not more Thanksgiving than four. Receiving and leveling describes a table that matches the number of chairs at it.

The failure mode is named just as plainly. In the chapter on painful disorders the Inner Canon lists how each emotion moves qi, and for grief the line is that grief disperses it (悲则气消, bēi zé qì xiāo). Not that sadness is forbidden. Grief scatters the small continuous effort required to cook, sit down, and eat off a plate, which is why a holiday dinner so often gets eaten standing over the sink at six.

Which gives the counter-instruction in one word: gather. One warm thing, one sour thing, a set time, a real plate. That is a whole Thanksgiving, and it works for one person as well as two.

## Four dishes, and the times that make them work

The bird: three-pound bone-in breast, three-quarters of a teaspoon of kosher salt per pound rubbed on tonight, uncovered in the refrigerator. That is a little over two teaspoons. Thursday, oven to 350°F, roast on a rack, and plan seventy-five to ninety minutes. Start checking with an instant-read at sixty-five. Pull at 165°F away from the bone and rest twenty minutes under foil. The temperature is the instruction; the clock is only a forecast.

The starch: two twelve-ounce sweet potatoes, 400°F, fifty to sixty minutes. The stuffing goes in when the bird is halfway done and comes out while the roast rests.

Run the clock: oven on at 2:40, breast in at 3:00, stuffing in at 3:40, breast out at 4:30, carve at 4:50. A two-hour afternoon, not a two-day project, and most of it is waiting.

## The sour dish is the last one to cut

Cranberry sauce is the first thing people drop when the guest list shrinks, and the tradition would keep it and drop something else.

Sour has a direction, and it runs opposite to sweet. The chapter on the seasons of the organs says the lung wants to gather and that sour is what gathers it (肺欲收，急食酸以收之，《素问·藏气法时论》). On a plate built from butter, cream, bread, and sugar, that is the one flavor pulling the other way, which is why a spoon of it resets the mouth after the third bite of stuffing.

Make it tonight, since it improves overnight: six ounces of berries, a quarter cup of sugar, a strip of orange peel, eight minutes. Fish the peel out before it goes in the fridge, and do not judge the texture while it is hot. Pectin sets as it cools.

Then go heavier on the black pepper than feels right. Pungent disperses (辛散), and on a small roast with a lot of exposed surface, the pepper does work that a bigger bird's mass would have done on its own.

## If it is already Thursday and nothing is thawed

Buy a rotisserie chicken. It is cooked, it is at temperature, and it costs less than the breast you were going to roast. Fifteen minutes in a low oven with broth brings the skin back. Open a jar of whole-berry sauce instead of making it. Roast one sweet potato, and make gravy from a cup of broth, a tablespoon of flour, and whatever fat is in the pan. Two people will not call that a compromise; it is the same four categories on the plate.

If the stores are shut and the refrigerator is empty, the pantry version is eggs, toast, frozen peas, and a can of soup, eaten at a table with the phone in the other room. The bird was never the ritual. One warm thing, one sour thing, sitting down at a stated time is the ritual.

And do not attempt a cold-water thaw on a fourteen-pound bird Thursday morning. Seven hours of changing water is not a plan.

Set the table before you cook, even if it is one place. A plate, a glass, a napkin, and a time you have told someone about. Ten minutes, and it is the whole difference between a Tuesday dinner and the holiday.

After the plates, walk to the end of the block. The cup for the evening is warm milk with turmeric and a pinch of black pepper, one mug, no dessert required behind it: [golden turmeric milk](/recipes/golden-turmeric-milk/).

If Thursday is an anniversary, or if this is the third holiday in a row spent alone and it has stopped feeling like a choice, that is not a menu problem and this page will not pretend otherwise. Cook the four dishes anyway, keep the standing call at four, and tell someone who can answer back about the rest.
