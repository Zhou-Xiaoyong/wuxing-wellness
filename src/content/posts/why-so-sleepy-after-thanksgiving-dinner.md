At 4:40 the plates are still on the table, the game is on with the volume down, and the person in the recliner has a napkin on his chest and one shoe off. Nobody put him there. The meal did.

Here is the short answer: it is not the turkey. It is the size of the plate, the hour it landed, and the ten minutes between the last bite and the chair. Change the last one and the other two stop mattering as much.

A body handles a large meal at one o'clock. The same meal at four, after ten hours awake, is a different event.

None of this is medical advice. If you get sleepy after most meals rather than this one, or you wake at two in the morning with a stomach that still feels full, that pattern belongs in an exam room and not in a smaller slice of pie.

## The dip was on the calendar before the bird went in

Alertness in most people drops in the early afternoon, somewhere between one and three. Sleep researchers have measured that trough in subjects who ate nothing at all, which is the part the holiday conversation always skips. It is a circadian dip, not a digestive one.

Thanksgiving then does something specific. It takes the largest meal of the year and drops it right on top of a low point that was scheduled months ago.

Add the shape of the day. Up at six to get the bird in. Coffee instead of breakfast, or a skipped lunch because dinner is at four. Two hours standing over a board of cheese. By the time the plates are full, most hosts have been awake ten hours and on their feet for half of them. The food is the fourth thing the body has been asked to handle.

That is why the slump arrives so fast after the last bite. It was already waiting.

## Where the blood went, in the tradition's terms

The Inner Canon describes the healthy direction of things in one compressed line from the great treatise on yin and yang correspondences (《素问·阴阳应象大论》): the clear yang emerges through the upper openings, the turbid yin goes down through the lower ones (清阳出上窍，浊阴出下窍).

Eyes, ears, mouth, nose. That is where clear yang is supposed to arrive, and it is why a settled person reads as bright in the face rather than dull.

A large meal redirects the work. For the next hour the body's attention sits below the diaphragm, and in the traditional reading the clear yang does not reach the head while that is happening. The result is not pain. It is a head packed with cotton, and limbs that seem to have been filled with sand.

The four limbs are Earth territory in this system, which is why heaviness rather than sharpness is the classic sign of a center that is either overloaded or simply tired. Telling those apart takes a week rather than an afternoon. If lunch puts you under the table on a Tuesday in March as well, the reading is a weak center rather than one large meal, and [the spleen's own page](/blog/earth-element-organ-spleen/) is the place to start.

## Lie Down on a Full Stomach (饱食即卧，乃生百病)

Sun Simiao, writing in 652, put the rule in eight characters in his thousand-ounce formulas (《备急千金要方》): lie down on a full stomach and a hundred illnesses arise (饱食即卧，乃生百病).

A hundred is rhetoric. The direction is the point. The tradition is not against rest after eating; it is against horizontal within the hour. Anyone who has fallen asleep after a late dinner and woken with acid in the throat has already tested the reasoning. Gravity is doing a job at the table that it cannot do on a couch.

There is a second cost, less physical. Lying down erases the signal that says the meal is over. At the table, the plate, the conversation and the upright posture all mark an ending. In a recliner with the game on, the mouth keeps finding reasons to go back to the kitchen, and the second plate at seven is usually eaten by someone who was asleep at five.

Twenty minutes upright covers most of it. Sitting at the table with a cup of tea counts. So does standing at the counter arguing about football.

## Volunteer for the plates

Here is the least popular useful advice in this piece. The ten minutes after the meal decide the evening, and the best thing to do with them is something mildly physical that has an end.

Clearing a table is that thing: eight trips between a table and a sink, which is more movement than most people get between two and nine on a Thursday.

The classical argument for movement after eating is short. In the chapter on the yang brightness vessel (《素问·阳明脉解》), the four limbs are called the root of all yang (四肢者，诸阳之本). Yang is what stands you up. Move the limbs and you move the thing that was pooling in the middle.

Ten minutes of easy walking after a meal does more for how you feel at six than twenty minutes of lying down, and it leaves no debt behind.

## If you are already under the blanket

It is 5:15, you slept for forty minutes, and you have woken up worse. This is the ordinary result. Sleep that deep, taken that early, ends in a groggy half hour, and then the real cost arrives at eleven, when you are wide awake and Friday starts in seven hours.

Take the recovery in this order.

Water first, a full glass, before coffee and before anything from the fridge. A salty meal and two glasses of wine leave most people short by a liter, and mild dehydration reads as fatigue rather than thirst.

Then outside, coat over the sweater, one lap of the block. The cold is unpleasant for ninety seconds and useful for the next three hours.

Set a coffee cutoff and keep it. Caffeine's half-life runs about five hours in most adults, so a cup at 4:30 still has half of itself in you at 9:30. If you want to be asleep by eleven, the last cup is at two.

If a nap is unavoidable, make it twenty minutes and set an alarm. Past half an hour you are into deep sleep, and waking from it costs more than the nap returned.

When the pie comes back out at eight, the slice is not the problem. The slice an hour before bed is a different Thursday, and Saturday will report on it.

One small substitution the tradition would recognize: a single hot cup of [ginger and red date tea](/recipes/ginger-jujube-tea/) at six, no sugar, instead of a third coffee. Warm, pungent, and light enough that it does not restart the meal. A cup, not a pot.

## Quick self-check

| Statement | Feels like me |
| --- | --- |
| I get sleepy after most meals, not just this one | ☐ |
| I skipped lunch and started eating at two | ☐ |
| The first thing I did after the last bite was sit down | ☐ |
| I have not been outside since the first guest arrived | ☐ |
| I plan to make coffee at five to get through the evening | ☐ |
| I nap on the couch and wake up foggier than I went down | ☐ |

Four or more and the fix is one thing, not five: stay upright for twenty minutes after the meal, then walk one block before you sit down for the night. Two or fewer, and your Thursday is shaped better than most.

One caveat worth stating plainly. Sleepiness after every lunch, or a stomach still full at two in the morning, is not a holiday problem, and a smaller plate will not answer it.
