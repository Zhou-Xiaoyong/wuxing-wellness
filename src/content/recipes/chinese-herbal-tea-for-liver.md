At four in the afternoon your eyes have that scraped feeling, the spot between your shoulder blades is tight, and the third coffee did nothing at all. In a Chinese kitchen this is not treated as a coffee problem. A small pot goes on the stove.

What goes in is not a tea bag. Toasted cassia seed (决明子, jué míng zǐ) simmers twelve minutes; chrysanthemum (菊花, jú huā) and goji (枸杞, gǒu qǐ) go in off the heat. Three ingredients, three jobs, and one cannot be steeped.

This is a ritual, not a treatment. If your eyes are gritty from eight hours of screens, the cup mostly buys you twenty minutes away from the screen.

## A simmer, not a steep

Nearly every liver tea sold in the West is an infusion: flowers in a mug, five minutes, done. That works on petals, not on a seed.

Cassia seed is a hard brown seed the size of a lentil, and its bitterness sits behind a coat hot water barely opens. Twelve minutes at a bare simmer, small bubbles at the edge of the pot and never a rolling boil, is what draws it out. Steep it like a flower and you get brown water.

Order matters as much as time. The aromatics in chrysanthemum, the part that feels cooling in your nose, leave with the steam.

## The three parts

A liver cup here is assembled from three roles; a single-herb tea is missing two. Cooling alone tastes like punishment.

| Role | Herb | Amount | When it goes in |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Cooling, moving down | Cassia seed (决明子, jué míng zǐ) | 1 tbsp, cracked | First, in cold water |
| Cooling, moving up | Chrysanthemum (菊花, jú huā) | 6–8 flowers | Off the heat, 3 minutes |
| Nourishing | Goji (枸杞, gǒu qǐ) | 1 tbsp | Off the heat, 3 minutes |

The directions are the interesting part. Chrysanthemum lifts clear yang to the head, which is why it turns up with eye strain and a heavy forehead. Cassia seed moves downward, which is why it appears alongside dry stools and the headache that sits at the top of the skull. The Compendium of Materia Medica (《本草纲目》) says the seed took its name from its work on the eyes, the characters reading as "opening clarity."

Goji is one of the few foods carrying zeaxanthin, the carotenoid concentrated in the macula. Cassia seed's main actors are anthraquinones, which shapes the caution below.

## Ingredients

- 1 tbsp toasted cassia seed (决明子, jué míng zǐ), about 8 g
- 3 cups cold water
- 6–8 whole dried chrysanthemum flowers (菊花, jú huā), white or yellow
- 1 tbsp dried goji berries (枸杞, gǒu qǐ)
- 1 strip dried tangerine peel (陈皮, chén pí), about an inch long (optional)
- A small piece of rock sugar (optional)

## Method

1. Crack the cassia seeds lightly with the back of a spoon or a mortar. You want the hulls split, not the seeds powdered.
2. Put the cracked seed and the tangerine peel, if using, in a small saucepan with the cold water. Boil, then drop to the lowest heat that still shows a few bubbles.
3. Simmer 12 minutes, lid ajar, until amber-brown and smelling of roasted grain. A hard boil makes it cloudy and harsh.
4. Take the pot off the heat. Add the flowers and the goji, lid back on, and wait exactly 3 minutes.
5. Strain into two mugs, pressing the goji against the strainer. Sweeten with rock sugar if the bitterness bothers you.
6. Drink it warm. Eat the softened goji if you like; leave the seeds behind.

## Which liver this is for

Two different pictures get called "liver," and only one wants this pot.

The first runs hot and stuck: red or dry eyes, a bitter taste on waking, a fuse that shortens through the day, a headache behind the eyes, stools gone dry.

The second is stuck without heat: sighing, a band of tightness around the ribs, a mood that lifts after a walk, no redness anywhere. That one wants something floral and moving, and the [Rose & Citrus Tea](/recipes/rose-tea-liver/) is the closer match.

## When to reach for it

- A week of double shifts at a desk, eyes gritty by mid-afternoon.
- The morning after a late, salty, fried dinner, with a bitter taste and a dull temple ache.
- Early spring, when the tradition says liver qi rises and tempers shorten.
- Any afternoon when the alternative is a fourth coffee.

## The caution that matters

Cassia seed belongs to the anthraquinone family, the same broad group as senna. Hence the constipation pairing, and why it is not a daily habit for months. Habitual use can leave you with loose stools and, over time, with potassium loss that matters if you are on a diuretic or digoxin. Skip it during pregnancy and breastfeeding, and skip it if your stools are already loose. If you take a blood thinner or a heart medication, ask a pharmacist first.

The traditional rhythm is five days on, two days off, stopping early if your digestion changes.

Start with one pot this week, on a day your eyes are tired and your temper is short, and see whether the twenty minutes is what helped.
